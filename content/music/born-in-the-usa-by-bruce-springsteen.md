## Why I Reacted To This Song
Few songs in American music get misread quite as consistently as this one and that irony is part of why it's stuck with me.

## About This Song
"Born in the U.S.A." was released in 1984 as the title track of Bruce Springsteen's album of the same name. Its stadium-sized chorus turned it into an instant anthem, famously misappropriated by politicians, including Ronald Reagan's 1984 campaign, as a patriotic rallying cry. The verses tell a very different story: a Vietnam veteran returning home to unemployment, neglect and a country that has no use for him.

## First Impressions
The chorus is enormous, practically built to be chanted, which is exactly what makes it so easy to mistake for celebration. Listen to the verses, though and the song is telling a much harder, sadder story.

## My Reaction
The song has spent decades caught between two wrong readings. One camp hears the chorus and waves it like a flag, treating it as pure patriotic celebration. Another hears the bleak verses and calls it an anti-America song, a takedown of the country. Both miss what's actually there. "Born in the U.S.A." is one of the most patriotic songs ever written, not because it praises the country, but because it's honest about where it's failing its own people. Loving a country blindly isn't patriotism; pointing at its real, obvious flaws and demanding better is. That's what this song does and it's why the misreadings, in both directions, say more about the listener than the song.
