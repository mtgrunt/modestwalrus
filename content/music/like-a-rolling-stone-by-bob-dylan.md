## About This Song
Bob Dylan released "Like a Rolling Stone" on July 20, 1965 through Columbia Records. Its biting lyrics grew out of a long piece of writing Dylan put together in June 1965, after coming home worn out from a demanding tour of England. He trimmed that draft down to four verses and a chorus, then recorded the song a few weeks later as the opening track of his sixth studio album, Highway 61 Revisited.

The sessions didn't come easy. Over two tough days of preproduction, Dylan struggled to find the heart of the song and early attempts in 3/4 waltz time went nowhere. Everything clicked once he switched to a full rock arrangement and a young session player named Al Kooper improvised the now famous Hammond organ riff.

## First Impressions
That snare crack opens the song and Kooper's organ sweeps in right behind it.

## My Reaction
"Like a Rolling Stone" sounds like a dare. Dylan sneers his way through every verse and the chorus lands like a challenge to the listener. Knowing that Al Kooper's organ part was improvised on the spot makes it even more remarkable.
