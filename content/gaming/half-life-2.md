## My Reaction
Half-Life 2 changed what a shooter could be. More than 20 years later, City 17 and the gravity gun are still unforgettable.

## Overview
Half-Life 2 is a first-person shooter developed and published by Valve and built on its Source engine. You play as Gordon Freeman, who is dropped onto a train into City 17 by the mysterious G-Man and soon joins the resistance fighting to free Earth from the alien Combine empire. Along the way Alyx Vance hands him the gravity gun, which lets you pull objects toward you or launch them across the room. It launched on Windows and later came to Xbox, Xbox 360, PlayStation 3, Mac, Linux and Nvidia Shield. Valve followed it with Lost Coast, Episode One and Episode Two, while Episode Three was canceled. The game won 39 Game of the Year awards and had sold 12 million copies by 2011. For its 20th anniversary in November 2024, Valve released a two-hour documentary about the making of the game.

## What I Liked
- The gravity gun turns physics into one of the most fun weapons ever made.
- City 17 is a grim, unforgettable setting.
- Alyx Vance is one of gaming's best companions.
- The pacing keeps throwing new ideas at you from start to finish.

## The Controversies
- Valve originally promised a 30 September, 2003 release, then delayed the game just a week before, which caused a fan backlash.
- In 2003 the game's source code was stolen by a German hacker and leaked online.
- Valve fought a legal dispute with its retail distributor Vivendi Universal Games, which ended with a 2005 settlement that stopped Vivendi from distributing Valve's retail games.

## Verdict
Half-Life 2 is one of the most important shooters ever made and it still holds up today. It's a must-play for anyone who loves games.
